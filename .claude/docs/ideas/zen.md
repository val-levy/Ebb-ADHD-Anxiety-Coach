# Zen garden

A screen of its own: a personal zen garden you tend over weeks and months. It's not a reset (resets are 90-second rescues). It's a slow, calming place you come back to, and it becomes *yours*.

## Why it works (psychology)
| Principle | Source | How the garden uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soft fascination restores attention | Attention Restoration Theory (Kaplan & Kaplan) | Raking sand is gentle, effortless attention. Patterns emerge without effort and there's no goal. |
| Rhythmic, repetitive movement calms | Research on repetitive motor activity and self-soothing (e.g. knitting, coloring studies) | A slow drag leaves rake lines; soft haptic ticks keep the rhythm |
| Autonomy + competence sustain habits | Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan) | You choose and place every element; nothing is scored |
| No loss aversion / guilt | Spec principle 5 (no streaks) | The garden never decays, wilts or scolds you for being away |
| Ownership increases attachment | IKEA effect / endowment effect | Your layout persists and grows richer the more you tend it |

The app makes no therapeutic claims (wellness wording only, per CLAUDE.md).

## MVP (now)
- A single sand garden that fills the screen
- Drag to rake: lines are drawn in the sand with soft haptic ticks
- Place elements (stone, moss, lantern, bonsai, water ripple) and drag to move them; long-press to remove
- "Smooth the sand" clears the rake lines and keeps the elements
- Saved to Supabase, so your garden is there next time
