# Calm guide (replaces the zen garden)

User idea (2026-11-10): walk the user through psychology-backed methods to get out of fight-or-flight during an **active panic attack**: breathing, body, environment, distraction. Like Peloton's guided sleep/meditation classes, but for panic.

## Flow
Intensity 0-10 (SUDS) → guided techniques with voice, haptics, breathing circle and segmented progress → intensity again → "what helped most?" (optional).

## Techniques and evidence
| Technique | Mechanism | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name it | Reframes symptoms as a harmless alarm, breaking fear-of-fear | CBT for panic (Clark 1986) |
| Cyclic sighing | Double inhale, long exhale; best mood/anxiety drop vs. other breathing | Balban et al. 2023, Cell Reports Medicine |
| Long exhale (4/8) | Vagal brake, slows heart rate | Slow-breathing / HRV research |
| Cold reset | Mammalian dive reflex | DBT TIPP (Linehan) |
| 5-4-3-2-1 grounding | Redirects attention to senses | Grounding / mindfulness |
| Tense & release | Burns off tension, signals safety | Jacobson PMR |
| Busy your mind | Working-memory load crowds out catastrophic thoughts | Distraction research |
| Change your space | Lowers sensory input | Sensory regulation |

## Personalization (MVP)
The most-picked "what helped" technique moves earlier in the panic program. Later: onboarding to pick preferences, adapt by intensity drop, and an offline queue.
